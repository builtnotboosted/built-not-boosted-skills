# Worked example: a short for a local bike workshop

An illustrative example. The business, the person and the customer moment are made up for teaching; in real use the intrigue beat comes from the person's actual week, never invented.

**Brief:** Sam runs an independent bike repair workshop. Audience: local commuters and weekend riders. Pillar: practical how-to. Topic: gears that skip or jump under load. Format: talking-head short filmed in the workshop, about a minute. Who-am-I and goodbye taken from brand-profile.

**Why it is built this way:**
- The hook names the topic plainly and promises a check, but does not say what the check is (no spoiler).
- The intrigue beat (a customer who bought new parts first) makes the viewer feel the problem.
- Facts are written in full (the ruler measurement), opinion beats are short.
- The close is one small thing to try tonight, with a question about that exact step.
- About 180 spoken words, so just under a minute at 190 words a minute.

## script.txt

```
<WALK IN>
So if your gears jump about every time you push up a hill, there's a two-minute check worth doing before you spend a penny.
I'm Sam, I fix bikes at the workshop on the high street. Give me a follow and let's get into it.

Had someone in this week who'd already bought a whole new set of gears online.
And the bike still skipped. Gutting.

<STEP CLOSER>
Nine times out of ten it's one of two things.
The cable's stretched a touch, which is a quick adjustment.
Or the chain's worn, and that's the one you can check yourself.

<PICK UP RULER>
Chains stretch as they wear. Twelve links of a new chain measure exactly twelve inches, pin to pin.
If yours measures twelve and an eighth or more, it's time for a new chain, and it's probably been chewing the gears too.

Catch it early and a chain is a cheap fix. Leave it, and it takes the expensive bits with it.

<HOLD COVER LOOK>
So grab a ruler and measure your chain tonight. How did yours measure? Let me know in the comments.
Catch you soon.
<B-ROLL: CHAIN CLOSE-UP, RULER, WORKSHOP DOOR>
```

## What the caption carries (written by the `writing` skill)

The plain-language hook, one line on what the video shows, the question again, and the contact line from brand-profile. No link in the caption on platforms where links are not clickable.

## What review-loop would check

Hook lands and is not the answer; who-am-I and follow straight after; intrigue delays the answer; the "two-minute check" promise is paid off; one try-this step with a matching question; goodbye on its own line; no "most people"; shot reminders on their own lines; on-screen hook is a promise; length roughly right for the pace; the fact is sourced.
