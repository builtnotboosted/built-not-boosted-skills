# Platform packaging

Last checked: Nov 2026, verify before relying on it. Platforms change limits and ranking behaviour often. When a number matters (a hard character limit, a link rule, a hashtag cap), check the platform's own help pages or your scheduler's docs on the day. Figures marked "reported" come from industry research, not the platforms themselves.

## What each platform is rewarding (Nov 2026)

- **Completion and watch time** decide short video everywhere. Build for sound-off with burned-in captions and the hook on screen in the first three seconds. Say the keyword aloud: TikTok and Instagram both use speech to categorise and rank for search.
- **Facebook:** Reels are the reach lever and plain feed images are throttled. Private shares (Messenger, WhatsApp) are weighted highest, then saves and comments. Native clips beat cross-posted or watermarked ones, so strip watermarks. Reels posted the day a trend breaks get extra weight (reported). The first few hours are the test window; reply to commenters fast, because a reply thread is one of the strongest organic signals.
- **Facebook link rationing:** from Sep 2026 Meta began limiting organic link posts on Pages without a paid subscription (reported around two free link posts a month, rolled out account by account; a first-comment link reportedly counts, extra links in the comments of a post that already has one reportedly don't). Treat links as a small monthly budget spent on the strongest piece, one per half-month if you have two. Check your Page for Meta's notice; your allowance may differ.
- **Instagram:** carousels earn the highest engagement and get re-shown to people who didn't finish them. DM shares weigh heavily for Reels. Keywords in the caption and profile now beat hashtags for discovery (following hashtags was removed). Reposted or watermarked content is de-ranked, and frequent reposting can drop an account from recommendations (reported). Public professional-account posts can appear in Google Search, so write captions that make sense to a searcher.
- **LinkedIn:** personal profiles out-engage company pages. Comments and saves drive reach far more than likes. The first hour matters. A link in the body cuts reach heavily (reported around 60%), and first-comment links are now detected and penalised too, so keep most posts link-free and point to the profile. Well-written native text posts compete with carousels; use carousels for genuine list or step content. Distribution filters for low-effort AI-sounding posts were reported in Sep 2026, which is one more reason to write in the person's own voice.
- **TikTok:** search is a primary discovery route, so lead with the keyword in speech, on screen and in the caption. A new video is tested on existing followers first, so early engagement gates reach to strangers. Stay on a consistent topic. Undisclosed brand content has reduced reach, so keep ad labels clean.
- **YouTube:** Shorts and long-form are ranked separately. For Shorts, watch-through on the first loop matters most; very short clips struggle to clear the watch-time bar. Viewer satisfaction surveys now carry heavy weight. Recycled hooks and formats are suppressed.
- **Google Business Profile:** post at least weekly (profiles quiet for 30 days lose visibility), reply to every review within about a day, and ask for reviews organically. Staff review quotas and soliciting reviews through named employees are banned. Questions that used to go in the Q&A tab now feed Google's AI answers through posts and reviews, so answer real customer questions in posts.

## Aspect ratios and safe zones

- Vertical wins almost everywhere: 4:5 (1080x1350) for feed stills, 9:16 (1080x1920) for video. Exceptions: Google Business 4:3 landscape, YouTube long-form 16:9.
- Design the master still at 4:5 with all text inside a central 3:4 safe zone, then derive other crops.
- **Grid-legible:** the profile grid shows the still, or the first frame of a video. Make sure the headline is legible in that frame, because new visitors judge the account from the grid.

## LinkedIn PDF carousels

LinkedIn shows document carousels in its own feed frame and stamps a title banner and arrows over each page. Why this matters: a carousel built on a 9:16 video template got centre-cropped in the feed and lost the first letter of its headline and half the logo.

- Build native at 4:5, 1080x1350. 1:1 is the safe fallback. Never reuse a 9:16 page.
- Keep the headline, logo, CTA and key figures in the central area, roughly 80px clear of every edge.
- Keep critical text out of the bottom 15 to 20% (the title banner) and clear of the far left and right (the arrows).
- The cover must read at thumbnail size on its own.
- PDF, 7 to 12 slides (8 to 10 is plenty), under 10MB.

## From one asset to every platform

**One still (4:5 master):** Instagram as is, or expanded into a 3 to 5 slide carousel. Facebook with a shorter question-led caption. LinkedIn as a PDF carousel or a native text post with the image. Google Business re-cropped to 4:3 with its own local caption and button.

**One short video (9:16 master):** TikTok native upload with a keyword-first caption; Instagram Reel with a short caption and hashtags in the first comment; YouTube Short with the keyword in the title; Facebook Reel with a conversational caption; LinkedIn native video, link-free.

Each version gets its own first line. Never the same hook pasted everywhere.

## File layouts

The `writing` skill writes two files into `content/<yyyy-mm-dd>-<slug>/`. Keep the words in one place (`captions.md`) so the scheduler, the reviewer and the person all work from the same copy.

### captions.md (the words)

```markdown
# Captions: <slug>

## Facebook

First line hook.

Value paragraph.

The question?

Disclaimer line, if any.

### First comment

Extra CTA, hashtags, plain-text contact.

## Instagram

...
```

Write captions exactly as they should appear, with real blank lines between paragraphs. No YAML or code fences around them, so they paste clean.

### PACKAGE.md (the facts about the piece)

```markdown
# Package: <slug>

format: static | carousel | animated | video | text
format_reason: one line
pillar: from the brand profile
disclaimer_tier: 0 | 1 | 2
affiliate: no | yes (partner name)

## Platforms
| Platform | Hashtags | Link | linkReason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook | 1 | none | none |
| LinkedIn | 0 | URL | article |

## Alt text
1. image-4x5.png: one plain sentence describing the scene and the baked headline.
2. slide-2.png: ...
```

`image-generation` adds its alt-text lines here; `scheduling` and `platform-data` read the format, links and pillar.
