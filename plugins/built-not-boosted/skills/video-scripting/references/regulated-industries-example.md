# Example: scripting for a regulated business (UK financial services)

This is one labelled example of how the method adapts when a business is regulated. It is not default behaviour. Your own rules live in brand-profile `## Compliance` and `## Boundaries`, and they win. If you are in a regulated industry, have your compliance function or regulator guidance confirm the specifics; this is not legal advice.

The example is a UK mortgage and protection adviser, regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Under FCA guidance on financial promotions on social media (FG24/1), every promotion must be fair, clear and not misleading, with balanced risk information, and the firm is responsible even for a throwaway line in a video.

## How the method changes

- **Risk warning in the caption, not spoken.** The adviser chose to carry the required risk warning in every post's caption (written by the `writing` skill from brand-profile's exact wording) and dropped the spoken risk line, so videos stay human and the warning is always present and exact. That choice was checked against their own compliance requirements. Some regimes or ad formats require the warning on screen or in the audio; if yours does, brand-profile says so and the script includes it.
- **Hooks keep tension in a real fact, never a promise.** "This clause in your offer could cost you thousands" is fine because the clause is real and checkable. "You'll definitely save" or "everyone should fix now" is the exact outcome-promise pattern regulators flag.
- **No hard-quoted prices on camera.** Rates and prices may move before the video airs, so spoken lines stay general ("there are good deals out there if you've got decent equity") and the exact figure, with its source and date, goes in the caption.
- **Permissions boundaries.** If the business cannot advise on something (in the example, a product it can only refer), the script may educate about it factually and signpost a referral, but never position the person as advising on or arranging it. List these in brand-profile `## Boundaries`.
- **Labelled illustrations.** Any worked figure is labelled illustrative in the caption and checked before publishing.
- **Different product, different closing question.** A video about one cover type should ask about that cover's own trigger, not a neighbouring product's, because a mismatched question can mislead.
- **Unregulated parts flagged.** Where some of the products discussed are not regulated, the caption says so, per brand-profile's wording.

## What stays the same

Everything else: the warm hook, the who-am-I, the intrigue, one idea, the try-this close, think-breaks, edit notes, and the voice loop. Compliance shapes the claims and where the warning sits; it does not have to make the video sound like a disclaimer.
