# Long-form: blog posts, LinkedIn articles, newsletters

Same voice as a caption, more room, more structure. The job is still one reader, one problem, one next step. Long-form also earns search traffic and gets quoted by AI answer engines, so a little structure goes a long way.

## Before writing

- Pick **one question** the reader actually types or asks. Use the words they use (the Audience section of the profile, `research`, or real customer questions), not your trade terms.
- Decide the **one thing** they should do after reading.
- Gather the facts from `brand/sources.md`. Any current figure gets a source and a `VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH` flag until checked on the day.

## Structure

1. **Headline:** the question or the answer, keyword in the first 50 characters or so. Set it before publishing, because some platforms (LinkedIn articles among them) lock the URL from the first headline. Research reported in Aug 2026 found LinkedIn articles with the target query near the start of the headline ranked in Google far more often.
2. **Opening, first two or three sentences:** answer the question plainly. Search engines and AI answers pull from here, and a reader who gets the answer early trusts you with the rest. Then the hook: why it matters to them.
3. **Body:** one idea per section. Subheads phrased as the questions a reader would ask next. Short paragraphs. A worked example or a real (true) story beats abstract advice. Lists where the content is a list.
4. **The honest other side:** when it doesn't apply, the trade-off, the catch. It builds trust and it's what separates advice from a sales page.
5. **Close:** one ask. A question to reply to, or a plain invitation to get in touch. No summary that repeats everything.
6. **Compliance:** the right tier from the profile, placed where it's read, not lost after a long footer.

Length: 500 to 2,000 words of genuine help covers most topics. Very long pieces (3,000 words plus) were reported to rank less often for LinkedIn articles; split them into a series instead.

## SEO basics (blog and website)

- **Title tag** around 60 characters, keyword first. **Meta description** around 150 to 160 characters: what they'll get, in plain words.
- **One H1** (the headline), then H2s for sections, H3s only if needed.
- **URL slug:** short, lowercase, hyphenated, the keyword.
- **Internal links:** two or three to related posts on the same site, with descriptive anchor text (not "click here").
- **External links:** to the primary source for any figure.
- **Images:** compressed, descriptive file names, alt text on each.
- **FAQ section** (optional): real questions, short answers. Can carry FAQ schema if the site supports it.
- **Freshness:** add a "last updated" date and actually update figures.
- **Author:** a real name and a line on why they know this. Expertise signals matter for search and for AI citations.

## LinkedIn articles

- Publish from the personal profile; individuals are cited and shown more than company pages.
- Keyword-led headline set first; answer in the opening lines; 500 to 2,000 words.
- A steady rhythm (say fortnightly) beats bursts.
- Links inside the article are fine. The promo post that shares it is on the link allow-list, with the link in the body.

## Newsletters and email

- Subject line: the hook, under about 50 characters, no clickbait.
- Preview text: the second line of the hook, not "View in browser".
- One story or idea, one link, one ask.
- Plain-text-looking emails often outperform designed ones for small senders.

## The promo posts

Every long-form piece gets a short post per platform, written by the main `writing` method:

- Hook from the article's most surprising line, not "New blog post!".
- One useful takeaway in the post itself, so it works even if nobody clicks.
- On the allow-list: the link goes where the platform prefers (body on LinkedIn, first comment on a Facebook link slot, bio on Instagram and TikTok).
- Off the allow-list (for example a Facebook post without a link slot): name the piece and point to the profile.

## Checks

The standard copy checklist in the `writing` SKILL.md, plus:

- Headline carries the keyword early and was set before publishing.
- The question is answered in the opening lines.
- Every figure has a source; current ones flagged until verified.
- Title tag, meta description, slug and alt text done for web posts.
- One ask at the end.
