# Writing

This skill owns how copy is written and packaged: the voice, the shape of a post, the format choice, per-platform packaging, links, the ask, disclaimers, affiliate labelling, first comments, hashtags, alt text and long-form articles. It hands visuals to `image-generation`, spoken scripts to `video-scripting` (this skill writes the caption that wraps the video), posting to `scheduling`, and quality checks to `review-loop`.

Authority order: `brand/brand-profile.md` wins on brand constants and compliance wording; this skill owns the method.

## Before you start

Read from the marketing workspace (default `marketing/`):

1. `brand/brand-profile.md`: Voice, Platforms (including the link allow-list), Boundaries, Compliance, CTA & contact. Open it every time; wording changes.
2. `brand/voice-learnings.md`: how this person actually writes. Read it before drafting anything, captions and headlines included, so the first draft already sounds like them.
3. `brand/performance-learnings.md`: what's working per platform, especially for format and hook choice.
4. `brand/sources.md`: where facts may come from, if the piece carries any.

No workspace? Say so in one line and offer the `brand-setup` skill. If the user wants to carry on, ask only the essentials: business, audience, platform, voice in a sentence, and whether they're regulated.

## The standard

Every piece grows the audience and moves someone one step closer to an enquiry or sale. It is true, compliant, sounds like a real person, and pastes clean with no fixing. Nothing ships until it's passed the checklist at the end and the `review-loop`.

## 1. The voice: a conversation over coffee

Write as if you're sat across a table from one nervous, intelligent person, not addressing a conference. Connect first, then teach: people remember how you made them feel long after they forget the detail.

- Contractions (you're, don't, we'll). Plain words. UK English unless the profile says otherwise.
- Vary sentence length hard. Short ones land. Then a longer one that takes its time and winds round to the point. AI copy flatlines at 15 to 20 words a sentence; people don't.
- Metaphors from everyday life instead of jargon. If a trade term is needed, explain it in brackets.
- Light humour where it lands (the profile says how much). Never forced, never at the reader's expense.
- Say the concrete thing you do, not the generic advice. "I'll pull the numbers for you and show you the three options" beats "get expert advice".
- Explain the why, not just the rule. Balance a claim with the honest other side; it reads as trust, not weakness.
- No em dashes. Use commas, brackets, colons or full stops. They're one of the loudest AI tells.
- Read it aloud. If it sounds like a brochure or a press release, rewrite it.

The full list of AI tells, word swaps and what to keep is in `references/human-writing.md`. Open it for the final pass on anything longer than a few lines, and whenever a draft "feels a bit AI".

## 2. The shape of a post

1. **Hook in line one.** A real hook, not a topic label: a question, a confession, a surprising fact, a stake, a curiosity gap, an identity call ("If you're about to..."). It has to work before the "see more" cut. On video, the hook lands in the first three seconds.
2. **Value in the middle.** One idea, taught well. Two or three concrete beats or a worked example. Don't cram.
3. **One ask.** A genuine question people want to answer, or a plain human invitation. One, not three (section 5).
4. **Compliance**, if the piece needs it, on its own paragraph (section 7).
5. **Contact**, where the platform allows (section 9).

## 3. Choosing the format

Format is a decision, not a default. Choose it for the message and the platform, then record it.

| Post intent | Default format |
|---|---|
| One punchy idea, a myth in a line, a timely alert | Animated static or short video |
| Step-by-step, checklist, comparison, process | Carousel (or a LinkedIn PDF document) |
| Human, local, community, a real story, networking | A real photo (don't animate real moments) |
| Low-stakes note; Google Business posts | Plain static |
| Teaching that needs a face and a voice | Video (script via `video-scripting`) |

Platform overrides worth knowing (detail and dates in `references/platform-packaging.md`):

- **Video out-reaches stills almost everywhere.** Judge a still on engagement (saves, comments, shares, profile taps), not reach. Why: in one long-running account the top twenty posts by reach were all video, while stills earned decent engagement on small reach. Scoring stills on reach would have killed formats that were doing their job.
- **Instagram and Facebook:** prefer motion or a carousel over a flat single image where motion adds something.
- **LinkedIn:** human posts stay real photos; teaching goes carousel or a well-written text post; fewer plain stills.
- **TikTok:** video first. Photo-mode carousels are reported to reach well, but test on your own account before relying on them. Why: one account found them at near-zero engagement over 28 days despite the industry claim.
- **Google Business Profile:** a plain landscape still; the words do the work.

Check `performance-learnings.md` before choosing: if a format has underperformed for this account, upgrade it; if a format isn't earning, rest it. Tag every piece in `PACKAGE.md` with `format:` and a one-line reason so `platform-data` can compare formats later.

## 4. Packaging per platform

Never paste one caption everywhere. Keep the idea and the asset, reshape the first line, length, hashtags and close for each platform. Open `references/platform-packaging.md` for the per-platform table (limits, hashtags, links, first comments, aspect ratios, hook style), the LinkedIn carousel safe zones and Google Business rules. It's dated; verify anything critical live before relying on it.

Vary the hook by platform: curiosity or a pattern-interrupt on TikTok and Reels, a warm question on Facebook, authority or insight on LinkedIn, local and factual on Google Business.

## 5. The ask

Reach is the means; an enquiry is the end. The route there is genuine engagement, not gimmicks.

- **End on one real question** people want to answer: their experience, their opinion, "what would you do?", a date or number from their life. Replies and saves lift natural reach, and on LinkedIn a comment or save is worth far more than a like.
- **Or a plain invitation**, where it fits: "if you want to talk it through, drop me a message".
- **No keyword CTAs.** No "comment GUIDE and I'll send it", no "DM the word READY". Why: they read as bait, platforms detect and demote "comment a word" asks, and they don't sound like a person. Offer the guide plainly through the profile or on request instead.
- **No engagement bait.** "Tag a friend", "like if you agree", "comment YES", vote-by-reaction. Meta lists these as bait.
- **Write to be sent.** Private shares are among the strongest signals on Meta's apps. "Send this to someone who's about to..." is fine because it's a genuine use. Make the piece worth forwarding: a checklist, a myth, a number people didn't know.

## 6. Links: profile-first, allow-list only

Most platforms now show link posts to fewer people, and some ration them outright. So the default on every platform is **no link**: the post stands on its own, ends on the question and sends people to the profile, where the bio link, buttons and pinned posts do the converting. "The links are on my Page", "link in bio", "the full video's on my YouTube".

A link goes in only for a reason on the profile's link allow-list (typically: an article promo, a long-form video release, an event that asks people to book, or one of a platform's rationed link slots). Record the reason in `PACKAGE.md` as `linkReason:`. Where it goes depends on the platform (in the body on LinkedIn, because a first-comment link mostly goes unseen; first comment on a Facebook link slot; never clickable on Instagram).

Write link-free at source. A link-free post must read naturally: no orphaned "click below" or "link in the comments". If a link gets caught later at scheduling, the piece comes back here for a rewrite, never a bare delete.

## 7. Compliance: right-sized, prominent

If the profile's Compliance section says the business is regulated or uses disclaimers, classify the post first, then match the tier from the profile:

- **Tier 0, none:** personal, behind-the-scenes, engagement and community posts.
- **Tier 1, short line:** general education that touches the regulated area but names no product, price, provider or offer. This is most everyday content.
- **Tier 2, full block:** promotes a specific product, price, offer, provider or incentive.

Use the exact wording from the profile, including per-platform variants. Never paraphrase it and never invent it; if a tier is `TODO`, hold the piece and flag it.

**Prominence beats completeness.** One clear line, on its own paragraph with a blank line above it, is read. A long block jammed onto the end of the CTA is skipped, and it turns the caption into a wall. Don't default to the longest wording "to be safe"; over-applying it is how warnings get buried. The disclaimer counts toward the character limit. Label any illustrative figure as illustrative.

`references/regulated-industries-example.md` shows tiering for one regulated industry, as a model. It is an example, not this user's rules.

## 8. Affiliate links, ads and your own products

**Any piece carrying an affiliate or partner link is an ad**, even a passing mention. Why: a post once went out with a partner link and no label because the rule was read as "only for dedicated ads". So every such piece carries all three, or it doesn't ship:

1. **"Ad" as the first line**, on its own, visible before any "see more" cut. Not "#spon", not "in association with", not an @mention. Where the first thing people see is a title, thumbnail or on-screen text, the label goes there too. (This follows the UK ASA's influencer guidance; check the rules where you are.)
2. **An honest price.** Never "free trial" alone: "free for 7 days, then £X a month, cancel anytime". Leaving the price out is a misleading omission. Re-check the live terms every time; they change.
3. **The commission disclosure**: "I may earn a small commission if you sign up through my link, at no extra cost to you."

Items 2 and 3 sit with the link. Gifted or paid mentions follow the same labelling. Promoting your own product or service on an educational channel: say plainly that it's yours, and make no outcome or income claims you can't back up.

## 9. First comments, hashtags, contact, alt text

- **First comments** (standard on Facebook and Instagram): the extra CTA, the hashtags and the contact block in plain text. Write them out in full; don't leave them to chance. No URL unless the post holds a link slot. Never put mandated compliance wording only in a comment.
- **Hashtags:** few and focused, all pointing at one topic, because mixed tags confuse categorisation. Counts per platform are in the reference. Don't double up between caption and first comment.
- **Contact block:** from the profile, per platform. Swap link lines for "links on my Page" or "in my bio" where links are rationed or dead.
- **Alt text:** one plain sentence per image and per carousel slide, describing the scene and any text baked into it. No hashtags, links or phone numbers. It's accessibility first, and it helps search. Video and text-only posts need none.

## 10. Formatting that survives the scheduler

Short paragraphs of one or two sentences, with a **genuine blank line between every paragraph**. The disclaimer and the contact block each get their own paragraph.

Why: some schedulers store captions as HTML paragraphs, and some feeds render adjacent paragraphs with no gap, so a nicely spaced caption arrived as one dense block (worse with a long disclaimer on the end). Real blank lines in the copy let the scheduler map each block to a spaced paragraph. Never write a run-on wall.

## 11. Truth

Never invent a story, client, event, figure, review, quote or result. Stories come from what actually happened, as the user tells it. Reviews are quoted verbatim from wherever the profile says real ones live. Figures come from a named source in `sources.md`; current figures in long-form get flagged `VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH`. No "studies show" or "9 in 10" without the source. If a slot calls for a story and there isn't a true one, teach the topic honestly instead. A slightly less dramatic true post beats an invented one every time.

## 12. Long-form: articles, LinkedIn articles, blog posts

Same voice, more structure. A keyword-led headline set before publishing (some platforms lock the URL from it), the answer in the opening lines, one idea per section with question-style subheads, 500 to 2,000 words of genuine help, one CTA, and a short promo post for each platform (the promo is usually on the link allow-list). Open `references/long-form.md` for the structure, SEO basics and the promo pattern.

## 13. Output and the baseline

Write into the piece folder, `content/<yyyy-mm-dd>-<slug>/`, using the layouts at the end of `references/platform-packaging.md`:

- `captions.md`: the words, one `## Platform` section each (caption, first comment, hashtags). This is the single copy of the words that `scheduling` posts from and the person edits.
- `PACKAGE.md`: the piece-level facts other skills read: `format` and its reason, disclaimer tier, affiliate yes/no, per-platform `link` and `linkReason`, and alt text per image (the `image-generation` skill adds its lines here too).

When the copy is final, also save an untouched copy of `captions.md` to `baselines/<yyyy-mm-dd>/<slug>-captions.md` (articles: `<slug>-article.md`). Never edit a baseline. Why: the user's own edits to your drafts are the richest voice signal there is, and the `voice-loop` skill learns by diffing what they posted against this as-generated copy.

## Checks before it ships

Run this, fix anything that fails, then hand the piece to the `review-loop` skill:

- Hook lands in line one, and differs per platform.
- One idea in the middle; one ask at the end, a genuine question or plain invitation. No keyword CTA, no engagement bait.
- Links only for an allow-list reason, `linkReason` recorded, placed correctly for the platform. Link-free posts have no orphaned "click below".
- Right disclaimer tier, exact profile wording, on its own paragraph (or correctly absent).
- Any affiliate or partner link: "Ad" first line, honest price, commission disclosure.
- Blank line between every paragraph.
- Platform limits honoured: characters, hashtag counts, title and tag limits (see reference).
- First comments written for Facebook and Instagram.
- Alt text for every still and every carousel slide.
- Format tagged with a reason.
- Nothing invented; every figure sourced.
- Voice: sounds like the person (check against `voice-learnings.md`), no AI tells, no em dashes, the profile's spelling locale.
- Inside the profile's Boundaries.

## Adapting it

- **Voice:** tune humour, formality and person in the profile; `voice-loop` keeps refining it from real edits.
- **Platforms:** add or drop rows in the profile; the packaging reference covers the common ones and the method carries to others (find the limits, the link rules and the native format, then reshape).
- **Regulated businesses:** fill the Compliance tiers with your exact wording; the tier logic here does the rest.
- **No scheduler:** `PACKAGE.md` is paste-ready by hand.
