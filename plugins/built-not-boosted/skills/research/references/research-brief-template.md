# Research brief template

The research brief is the hand-over between `research` and the skills that make the piece (`writing`, `video-scripting`, `image-generation`) and check it (`review-loop`). Keep the headings exactly as below so those skills can find what they need. Save it as `content/<yyyy-mm-dd>-<slug>/research-brief.md`, where the date is the planned publish date.

Write it plainly. It is a working document, not a post, so short lines and tables are fine. Leave a section in with "none" rather than deleting it, so the reader knows it was considered.

---

```markdown
# Research brief: <working title>

**Planned publish date:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Platforms:** <e.g. Instagram reel, LinkedIn post, YouTube Short>
**Pillar:** <from brand-profile ## Content pillars>
**Kind:** evergreen | live | dated
**Use by / expiry:** YYYY-MM-DD or "none (evergreen)"
**Researched:** YYYY-MM-DD by Claude (research skill)

## The question
The one question this piece answers, in the audience's own words if possible.
Where that wording came from (search suggestion, Search Console query, a comment, a DM, a forum thread), with date.

## Who it is for
The two or three audience types it speaks to, and what each needs to hear.
e.g. "someone booking for the first time" / "a regular who has never tried X".

## Why now
Demand signal, news peg, release, season or deadline. "Evergreen, no peg" is a valid answer.

## Key points
3-6 plain-English points the piece must land, in a sensible order.
Each point references the claim IDs that support it, e.g. "(C1, C3)".

## Claims table
| ID | Claim (as it may be said) | Source (publisher, title, URL) | Tier | Published | Checked | Flags |
|----|---------------------------|--------------------------------|------|-----------|---------|-------|
| C1 | ... | ... | 1 | YYYY-MM-DD | YYYY-MM-DD | VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH |
| C2 | ... | ... | 2 | YYYY-MM-DD | YYYY-MM-DD | illustrative |

Flags to use:
- `VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH`: a live figure or rule that can move; re-check on the day.
- `illustrative`: an example made up to explain, which must be labelled as illustrative wherever it appears.
- `person's own`: a story or fact the person supplied; note when they supplied it.
- `caveat`: there is a limit on how it can be used (see notes).

## Story material
Only real material: the person's own documented experiences (note when they gave it) or a published case study (cite it). Give the anonymised shape and stick to what happened.
If none fits: "None. Teach the topic directly."

## Angle and hook seeds
2-3 possible openings, each keeping the tension in a true fact rather than a promised outcome.
The "what this means for you" turn, split by audience type.
A genuine question the piece could end on, to invite real comments.

## Words to use
Search phrases and terms the audience uses (for titles, captions, spoken keywords).
Jargon to avoid or explain.

## Handle with care
Figures or framings that are true but mislead out of context, claims the brand never makes (from ## Boundaries), compliance lines that must appear (from ## Compliance).

## Open questions
Anything unverified, missing, or that needs the person's input. Nothing here goes into the piece as fact.

## Sources
Full list, one line each, so anyone can check every claim:
- <Publisher>, "<Title>", <URL>, published YYYY-MM-DD, checked YYYY-MM-DD
```

---

## Notes for the consuming skills

- `writing` and `video-scripting` take the key points, angle and words from here, and may only state as fact what appears in the claims table.
- Anything flagged `VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH` is re-checked by whoever schedules or publishes; if it has moved, update the piece and the brief, or drop the figure.
- `review-loop` checks the finished piece against the claims table. A fact in the piece with no claim ID behind it is a fail.
- If the brief is older than its expiry date when the piece is finally made, re-run `research` on the live claims first.
