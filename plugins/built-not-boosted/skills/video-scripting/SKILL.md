# Video scripting

This skill owns the spoken script and its edit notes. It writes what the person says on camera, in their voice, shaped to hold attention, and hands three short lines to the `video-editing` skill so the edit knows the on-screen hook, the cover words and the promised outcome. Captions and post copy belong to the `writing` skill, format choice and topic research to `research`, the edit itself to `video-editing`, and QA to `review-loop`.

The core belief: **a script is a skeleton for a real person, not a read-aloud.** People who sound good on camera explain around the page. Write for that, and the video sounds like them instead of like an advert.

## Before you start

1. Find the marketing workspace (default `marketing/`). If none, say so in one line and offer `brand-setup`, or ask the essentials: who is speaking, what they do, who for, and where.
2. Read `brand/brand-profile.md`: `## Identity`, `## Audience`, `## Voice`, `## Boundaries`, `## Compliance`, `## CTA & contact`. Brand-profile wins on brand constants and compliance wording; this skill owns the method.
3. **Read `brand/voice-learnings.md` first, every script.** It is the record of how this person actually talks on camera, learned by the `voice-loop` skill from their filmed videos and hand-edits. Apply its current working rules; they beat the general guidance here wherever the two differ.
4. Read `brand/performance-learnings.md` for what has worked (formats, hooks, lengths) if it exists.
5. Get the facts. Any claim, figure or quote comes from the person, the brief or `research` with a source. Never invent a customer story, a figure, a quote or a review. If there is no real story, make it honest education instead.

## The standard

- **Warm and relaxed, not frantic.** The opposite of hard-cut, high-energy shouting. Inviting, unhurried, human. Light humour where it lands naturally, never at a customer's expense.
- **Sounds like them.** If a line would not come out of their mouth, rewrite it. Plain words, contractions, varied sentence length.
- **Stand beside the viewer, not in front of them.** Avoid "most people get this wrong" and "people often think", which tell the viewer they are probably wrong. Say "it's easy to miss", "a lot of us were told". Avoid hollow hype signposts ("here's the bit nobody tells you") that make the viewer wait for a payoff; introduce the real point plainly.
- **Useful, never a sell.** Build trust and the following; let enquiries come from that.
- **One idea per short.** Real problems your audience actually has, not fluff.
- **No em dashes** in script text. Commas, brackets, full stops.

## Structure: shorts (30 seconds to about 2 minutes, always under 3)

A shape, not a formula. Many creators will recognise it as Hook, Intrigue, Deliver, Exit; we do it the warm way.

1. **Hook.** One full spoken sentence, roughly 10 to 14 words, that names the topic plainly and gives the human stake or the honest twist. Not a staged scene, not a riddle, not "hi guys", not a logo. The tension lives in the fact, not in a hyped delivery. Write it as the whole sentence they would say (many people naturally open with a run-in like "So,"); clipped fragment questions tend to get rewritten on camera.
2. **Who-am-I and follow, straight after.** One short beat in the person's own words: name, what they do, for whom, and where if local, then a light follow ask. Example shape: "I'm Sam, I fix bikes at the workshop on the high street. Give me a follow and let's get into it." Keep the identity line consistent across videos (that is the point of it); vary only the tail so a batch filmed in one afternoon does not open identically. Put the location here, not in the hook. Never an outcome promise, never begging ("smash that follow").
3. **Intrigue.** One or two sentences, often from their real week ("had someone in this week who..."), that make the viewer *feel* the problem. Its job is to delay the answer, never to give it.
4. **Deliver.** The one clear idea, unhurried, in plain words. Pay off exactly what the hook promised; if the hook numbers or withholds something, write the payoff first and make sure the body cashes it in.
5. **Exit.** One small thing to try today, with a question about it: "Grab a ruler and check your chain tonight. How did yours measure? Let me know in the comments." One thread, one ask, phrased as "this helped me", never "you must". Then a quick, human goodbye on its own line, in their words (keep it the same every time once you know what they say).

**No-spoiler rule:** neither the hook nor the first line after the who-am-I may give the answer away. If it does, the viewer has what they came for and scrolls on.

## Structure: long-form (about 8 to 15 minutes, 16:9)

1. **Warm open:** the hook (the promise of the video), a light who-am-I for new viewers, and if you like a soft subscribe reason ("if this is useful, stick around, there's more like it").
2. **Setup:** frame the problem and say what the viewer will get.
3. **Chapters:** each a mini setup, teach, payoff. Make every transition a small hook forward rather than a stopping point, and plan two deliberate re-engagement beats around the one-third and two-thirds marks. Chapter markers in the script are silent markers for the edit; viewer-facing chapters come from the description timestamps.
4. **Payoff and ending:** resolve the promise, then exactly three asks: one thing to try, a pointer to a related video of theirs, and one comment question. Then a proper close.
5. **Writing order:** payoffs first, then setups, then tension, hook last, so the hook honestly matches what the video delivers.
6. **Search:** title and description lead with the viewer's question in their words ("why did my boiler pressure drop?") rather than the mechanism.

For reactive scripts (a news story, a price change, a data release), and more hook patterns and retention detail, open `references/hooks-and-retention.md`.

## Length and pace

- **Length follows the story.** Do not pad to a runtime and do not cut a real explanation to hit 40 seconds. Short shorts (30 to 60 seconds) and fuller 1 to 2 minute pieces are both fine when the story is told.
- **Size by pace.** Until voice-learnings holds the person's measured pace, assume about 190 words a minute: about 95 words for 30 seconds, 190 for a minute, 380 for two. Deliberate one-point pieces run slower (around 150), brisk round-ups faster (around 220). About 500 delivered words is a practical ceiling for a short.
- **Write close to the spoken length.** Early on, some people expand a script to twice its length on camera; once the delivered-to-written ratio in voice-learnings settles near 1.0, write at full length. Let the `voice-loop` numbers decide.
- **Facts in full, opinions as bullets.** People read facts and quotes almost word for word but riff on opinion beats, so write the checkable facts and any quote in full and leave opinion beats as short prompts.
- **Write plainer than feels satisfying on the page.** Ornamental phrases get replaced on camera with the plain version. Save the craft for one homely metaphor and the payoff line; concrete, everyday images survive filming best.

## Compliance

Brand-profile `## Compliance` decides. If the business is regulated or the video promotes something with required wording, the default is that the required warning rides in the **caption** (written by `writing`), not spoken, so the script stays human and the caption carries the exact words. Check your own rules: some regimes or ad policies require the warning on screen or spoken, and then brand-profile says so and you follow it. Either way, keep tension in real facts, never promise an outcome ("you'll definitely save"), and do not hard-quote a price or figure on camera that may change before it airs. A labelled example for UK financial services is in `references/regulated-industries-example.md`.

## Output (one folder per piece)

In `content/<yyyy-mm-dd>-<slug>/`:

1. **`script.txt`**, teleprompter-ready: spoken text only, clean line breaks, no headings, no em dashes. Think-breaks (a blank line every two or three sentences and before the payoff) so the speaker can glance away and come back, which also gives the edit clean cut points. Three to six shot reminders on their own lines in angle brackets and capitals, two or three words each, just before the line they apply to: `<WALK IN>`, `<STEP CLOSER>`, `<HOLD COVER LOOK>`, `<B-ROLL: KEYS, DOOR>`. Details and the PromptSmart example in `references/prompter-and-edit-notes.md`.
2. **`edit-notes.txt`**, three lines, never on the prompter:
   ```
   HOOK ON SCREEN: Gears slipping?
   COVER: CHECK THIS FIRST
   OUTCOME: you'll know the two usual causes of skipping gears and how to check one tonight
   ```
   HOOK ON SCREEN is 3 to 5 words that promise without giving the answer. COVER is 2 to 4 true, plain words for the thumbnail. OUTCOME is what the viewer gets; if you would not want it as a viewer, do not make the video.
3. **`sources.txt`**: where every fact came from.
4. **Baseline copy:** save an untouched copy of `script.txt` to `baselines/<yyyy-mm-dd>/<slug>-script.txt`. Never edit it. The `voice-loop` skill diffs the person's edits and their filmed delivery against it, which is how the next script gets closer to them.

Captions and titles for the posts are then written by `writing` from the script and edit notes.

## Checks before it ships

Pass the script and edit notes to `review-loop`, or check: hook lands in the first seconds and is not a spoiler; who-am-I and follow early, no outcome promise; intrigue delays the answer; every promise in the hook is paid off; one try-this step with a question about it; quick goodbye; no "most people", no hype signposts; on-screen hook is 3 to 5 words and not the answer; think-breaks and shot reminders present; length sized to the pace; no invented story or figure; compliance per brand-profile; voice-learnings rules applied; no em dashes.

## Adapting it

- **Your words:** put the person's who-am-I line and goodbye in brand-profile `## Voice` or `## CTA & contact`, and let `voice-loop` refine them from real footage.
- **More than one presenter:** give each their own who-am-I and keep their voice-learnings separate if they talk differently.
- **Your prompter:** any teleprompter app works; check once that its voice tracking scrolls past shot-reminder lines cleanly.
- **Your boundaries:** anything you cannot advise on or claim goes in brand-profile `## Boundaries`; scripts can educate about it and signpost, never position you as doing it.
