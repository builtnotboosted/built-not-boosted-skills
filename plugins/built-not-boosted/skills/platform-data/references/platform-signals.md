# Platform signals, timing and caps

**Last checked: Nov 2026. Verify before relying on it.** Platforms change ranking and reporting often, and much of what is published about algorithms is vendor research or folklore. Treat every figure here as "reported", use it to choose what to test, and let the person's own data in `brand/performance-learnings.md` win wherever the two disagree. When a decision hangs on one of these facts, check the platform's own help or creator pages first.

## Across platforms

- **Completion and watch time lead for video.** Platforms push videos people finish and rewatch. Build for sound-off (captions burned in, hook on screen in the first three seconds).
- **Active signals beat passive ones.** Saves, shares (especially private sends) and thoughtful comments move distribution far more than likes. Read these first in the review.
- **The first hour is a test.** A new post is typically shown to a small batch first and judged on early engagement before wider distribution: reportedly the first 30-60 minutes for TikTok and Reels, a few hours for YouTube Shorts, the first hour for LinkedIn. Posting 30-45 minutes before the audience's peak lets that first test resolve as the crowd arrives. A hypothesis worth testing, not a law.
- **Existing followers increasingly gatekeep reach.** On some platforms (TikTok reported Oct 2026), if core followers do not engage early, reach to strangers drops. Replying to early comments quickly matters.
- **Original beats recycled.** Watermarked, reposted or near-duplicate content is de-ranked on Instagram, Facebook and YouTube. Strip watermarks; vary hooks.
- **Search is a discovery channel on social too.** TikTok, Instagram and YouTube read spoken words, on-screen text and captions for search. Say the keyword aloud.

## Per platform

**TikTok.** Reported completion bar for wide distribution has risen (around 70%+). A couple of specific hashtags beat a pile of generic ones. Creators can suggest or block the keywords TikTok assigns to a video. Undisclosed brand content has its reach reduced, so keep ad and affiliate disclosure clean. Staying in a niche helps; scattering topics hurts. *Lesson from real data: photo-mode carousels were widely reported to get several times the reach of video, and one small account's own 28-day data showed them at roughly zero engagement. Test reported "hacks" on your own account before building around them.*

**Instagram.** Reels rank on watch time, replay rate and DM sends (sends reportedly weighted most for distribution). Carousels are the highest-engagement format for education. Hashtags capped at 5; keywords in captions and profile now beat hashtags for discovery. Trial Reels test a hook on non-followers. Heavy reposting can remove an account from recommendations. Public posts from professional accounts can appear in Google search. **Grid legibility matters:** the profile grid shows each video's first frame, so a headline that appears only mid-clip leaves a blank-looking tile, and those tiles have been observed to under-reach. Put the headline in frame one.

**Facebook.** Reels are the reach lever over photos; native uploads beat cross-posted clips. Private shares are the most heavily weighted action. Same-day Reels on a breaking trend or event are reportedly surfaced more. **Links are rationed:** under Meta's 2026 subscription tiers, Pages reportedly get a small number of free organic link posts a month (around two at the base tier), and Instagram post links became a paid feature. Spend link posts on the strongest pieces and write everything else for a profile visit, a follow, a share or a message. Check the Page for Meta's own link-limit notice.

**LinkedIn.** Thoughtful comments and saves drive reach far more than likes, and the first hour decides most of it. A link in the post body reportedly cuts reach; many accounts now post link-free and drive to the profile, adding a link only when the post exists to send people somewhere (an article, a long video, an event booking). Personal profiles out-engage company pages. The ranker learns the two or three topics you post about and shows you to people interested in them, so off-topic posting dilutes reach. Strong posts can keep resurfacing for two or three weeks, so picking the right piece matters more than the exact minute. Native text posts can match carousels; use carousels for genuine list or step content.

- **The weekly cap idea.** Rather than cross-posting everything, cap LinkedIn at a few strong posts a week (one small account settled on 3-5, target 4) and choose them in a **weekly pick**: rank all LinkedIn-suited candidates (personal and authentic, local and community, a genuine question, real teaching) and post only the best, at most one a day. Fewer, better posts protect topic authority and give each one room. Drop promo, straight news and filler from LinkedIn. Treat the number as a starting point and let the data move it.

**YouTube.** Shorts and long-form are ranked separately; read them separately. Shorts reportedly do best around 30-45 seconds (very short ones struggle to clear the watch-time bar), and YouTube has said viewer-satisfaction surveys carry more weight than raw watch time. Repetitive hooks and formats are filtered. Long-form builds searchable authority and is widely cited in AI answers; front-load the question and answer in the description.

**Google Business Profile.** Review recency and response speed weigh heavily in local ranking; a profile with no new posts or photos for 30+ days loses visibility. Insights now split impressions by surface (classic panel, Maps, AI summaries). Never incentivise reviews or set staff quotas.

## Best times to post: where they come from

There are three sources, and they say different things:

1. **The platform's follower-activity data** (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook show when followers are online). This describes the audience. Trust it most.
2. **A scheduler's best-time grid** (Publer and others). This describes when your own past posts did well. It is biased by when you happened to post, and one viral post can make a whole cell look like a peak. Use it to generate hypotheses.
3. **Generic "best time to post" articles.** Averages across millions of accounts that are not yours. A starting point for a brand-new account only.

When sources disagree (for example, the scheduler says 18:00 and follower activity peaks at 21:00), do not just switch: run a rotating test (see the main skill) for four to six weeks and read it by median. Record the current read and any live test in the Timing section of `brand/performance-learnings.md`, which is where the `scheduling` skill looks.

Posting time is a multiplier, not a lever on its own. A strong piece in an average slot beats a weak piece at the perfect minute.
