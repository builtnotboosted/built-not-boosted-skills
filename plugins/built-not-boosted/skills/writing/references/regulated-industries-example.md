# Regulated industries: a worked example

This file is an **example**, not the default. It shows how one regulated industry tiers its disclaimers so you can see the pattern and apply it to your own. The rules that actually apply to a user's content are the ones in their `brand/brand-profile.md` Compliance section, confirmed by them or their compliance contact.

Nothing here is legal or compliance advice. Wording and rules change; your regulator and your compliance contact have the final say.

## The pattern

Most regulated businesses over-apply disclaimers. The longest wording goes on every post "to be safe", captions become a wall, and nobody reads the warning. A tiered approach is both more readable and more compliant, because the warning that appears is actually seen.

1. **Classify the post** before choosing wording. Does it promote a specific product, price, provider, offer or incentive? Or is it general education? Or is it personal and not about the regulated service at all?
2. **Match the tier.** None, short, or full.
3. **Make it prominent.** On its own paragraph with a blank line above it, inside the caption (not only in a comment), counted in the character limit.
4. **Label illustrative figures** as illustrative, and get them checked before scheduling.

## Example: UK financial services (mortgage and protection adviser)

This is how a UK mortgage and protection adviser might set up the Compliance section. The wording reflects standard UK risk warnings; a real firm uses its own network's or compliance team's approved text.

| Tier | When | Example wording |
|---|---|---|
| 0, none | Personal, behind the scenes, community, engagement posts that don't touch a product | none |
| 1, short | General education: a myth-buster or tip that names no rate, product, lender, deal or incentive. Most everyday posts | "Your home may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments on your mortgage." |
| 2, full | Promotes a specific rate, product, lender, deal or incentive | "Information is for guidance only and does not constitute financial advice. Mortgage and protection products are subject to eligibility, underwriting and lender/insurer criteria. Your home may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments on your mortgage." |

Variants the same firm might hold:

- **Short platforms** (Instagram, TikTok): a compressed full tier, for example "Mortgage & protection products subject to status, underwriting and criteria. Your home may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments."
- **Insurance-only posts:** never the repossession line; instead something like "Insurance cover is subject to eligibility, underwriting and insurer criteria, and policies have exclusions and limitations."
- **Buy-to-let:** the standard tier plus "Most buy-to-let mortgages are not regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority."
- **Google Business Profile:** its own short line, with no phone numbers or links in the text.
- **Video:** some firms carry the warning in the written caption rather than a spoken line, so the caption must always have it. Never hard-quote a rate on camera that may have moved by the time it airs.

Other lessons from this example that carry to any regulated business:

- **Permissions matter.** Never position the adviser as doing something they aren't authorised to do. Educate and signpost a referral instead.
- **Fair, clear and not misleading** sits above any hook. A punchy line that overstates a benefit or hides a risk fails, however well it performs.
- **Stories and reviews** must be real, documented and anonymised as required; reviews quoted verbatim from the approved store only.

## Other industries, briefly

The same tiering works elsewhere. What changes is who sets the rules and what triggers them.

- **Health and wellbeing** (physio, nutrition, aesthetics, fitness): advertising rules on health claims apply. No cures, no guaranteed outcomes, no "pain-free"; claims need evidence. A short "general information, not a diagnosis" line is a common tier 1. Before-and-after images and testimonials have their own rules.
- **Legal and accountancy:** professional body rules on advertising, fees transparency and not implying outcomes.
- **Property and lettings:** material information rules on listings; fees shown clearly.
- **Alcohol, gambling, food supplements, children's products:** specific advertising codes with audience and claims restrictions.
- **Anyone using affiliate links, gifted products or paid partnerships:** ad labelling rules apply whatever the industry (see the `writing` SKILL.md, section 8).

If a user is regulated and has no approved wording, don't write it for them. Mark the tier `TODO: confirm with compliance` and hold any piece that needs it.
