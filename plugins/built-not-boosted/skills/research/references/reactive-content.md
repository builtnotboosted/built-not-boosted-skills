# Reactive content: news and scheduled releases

Some of the best-performing content a small business can make is a quick, honest "here is what just happened and what it means for you" piece, posted while the story is live. It works because people are already looking for an explanation, and most of what they find either reports the headline or shouts about it. A calm translation into plain English, aimed at the person watching, is genuinely useful.

This file covers how to do it without getting it wrong.

## Two kinds of event, two speeds

**Fast: simple numeric releases.** An index, a price print, a monthly figure, a published ranking. The number is instantly understandable, so the value is being early. Prepare the angle in advance, verify the actual figure the moment it lands, and turn the piece round within the hour if you can.

**Dig-in: complex changes.** A new rule, a budget, a regulation, a scheme, a platform policy. You cannot credibly react in the first ten minutes because you have to read what was actually announced and work out what it means for your audience. Read the primary document, pin down the parts that affect your people, then post the same day or next morning.

*Why the split matters: "first and wrong" on a policy detail does more damage than "a few hours later and right". Speed is a virtue for numbers you can check in a minute, not for documents that take an hour to read.*

## Keep a release calendar

Many of the events that move an audience are on a known calendar: official statistics, industry indices, quarterly reports, annual surveys, regulatory deadlines, seasonal peaks, trade shows, product launch weeks. List the ones that matter to this business in the calendar section of `brand/sources.md` with publisher, usual timing and kind (fast or dig-in).

- **Pull dates live each time.** Release schedules shift. Check the publisher's own calendar page; never trust a date copied into a file months ago.
- Note the time of day where it matters (some releases land at 07:00, some at midnight, some at noon), because that decides when you can post.

## Before the day: leave a hole and pre-prep

- **Leave a gap in the plan.** If you batch-create content, do not pre-make the piece a due release will cover. Mark that slot "reactive: <release> due <date, time>" so everything else gets made and that one slot stays open.
- **Pre-write the frame.** Draft the hook shape, the "what this means for you" split by audience type, and the closing question, with the figure left blank. On the day you drop in the verified number and adjust the direction.
- **Know your sources.** Have the primary publisher's page bookmarked so you verify against the original, not someone's tweet about it.

## On the day: verify, then translate

1. **Get the figure from the primary source.** Not from a headline, a round-up or a post. Record publisher, release name, time checked.
2. **Check it means what you think.** Monthly or annual? Seasonally adjusted? Revised from last time? Which region or group?
3. **Build the piece in this shape:**
   - Hook on the freshness and the stakes, not the statistic ("This morning's numbers are out, and here is what they actually mean for you").
   - Give the number once, plainly. Nobody feels a percentage; do not dwell on it.
   - Turn straight to "what this means for you", split by the two or three types of people in your audience.
   - One next step, or a genuine question that invites a real comment.
4. **Keep it direction-neutral where the future is uncertain.** Report what happened; do not forecast what happens next as if it were fact.
5. **Mark the brief `live`, with an expiry,** and flag every figure `VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH` for whoever schedules it.

Speed beats polish here: a same-day, slightly rough piece posted while the story is live tends to outperform a polished one two days later. But accuracy beats speed. If a figure cannot be verified, drop it.

## How big is "big"? Use tiers

Not every story deserves a reaction. A simple tier stops "reactive" turning into "twitchy":

- **Major:** genuinely changes something for your audience. Worth a reactive piece, and worth reviewing anything already queued that would now look tone-deaf (a breezy "prices are steady" post on the day prices jumped). Move a clashing queued post later rather than deleting it, unless the person says otherwise.
- **Watch:** notable, not drop-everything. Feeds into the next planned piece or the weekly round-up. No extra post.
- **None:** an ordinary day. Run the plan.

Set a volume cap (for example, at most one extra reactive post a day) so a busy news day does not flood the feed, and let a weekly round-up carry the rest.

## Guardrails

- The brand profile's compliance lines still apply in full, however urgent the story.
- Never react with anything naming a real private individual, a customer, or an unverifiable claim about a competitor.
- Naming the organisation that made the news is fine when it is factual commentary; present it fairly and never imply an endorsement.
- Do not double-post: check what is already queued before adding a reactive piece.
- Unscheduled breaking news is harder. If there is no capacity to react the same day, next morning is fine; say so rather than rushing.

## Where to put the lesson

After a reactive piece has run for a week, `platform-data` will show whether it earned its place. Record what worked in `brand/performance-learnings.md` so the next release day starts from evidence.
