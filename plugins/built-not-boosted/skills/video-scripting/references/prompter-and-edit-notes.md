# Teleprompter output, edit notes and shoot habits

Open this when preparing scripts for a filming session.

## Why the script is formatted this way

Most people who film themselves record fast and sort it out in the edit. So the prompter text carries everything they need on the day and nothing they do not, and a few lines of edit notes carry what the edit needs. No storyboards: shot variety, cutaways, graphics and the cover are designed in the `video-editing` skill.

## script.txt rules

- Spoken text only, in natural language for the brand-profile locale (for example UK English).
- Clean line breaks, no headings, no bold, no bullet marks, no em dashes.
- **Think-breaks:** a blank line roughly every two or three sentences and before the payoff. The speaker can glance away as if remembering and come back to the lens, which looks natural, and each pause becomes a clean cut point.
- **Shot reminders:** three to six per short, each on its own line, in angle brackets and capitals, two or three words, placed just before the line they apply to. They give direction, never dialogue. Common ones: `<WALK IN>`, `<STEP CLOSER>`, `<TURN, POINT>`, `<SIT DOWN>`, `<HOLD COVER LOOK>`, `<WALK OUT>`, and at the end `<B-ROLL: KEYS, DOOR>` for what to film before leaving.
- **Long-form chapter markers** go on their own line in square brackets, for example `[CHAPTER 2: THE QUOTE]`. They are silent: the speaker pauses on them and the edit removes the pause.
- The `video-editing` skill ignores any line wholly in `<...>` or `[...]`, and so does the `voice-loop` diff, so directions never pollute captions or voice learning.

**Teleprompter example: PromptSmart.** PromptSmart's editor has a Tags feature (the `<>` button) that uses exactly this angle-bracket format, and its voice tracking scrolls as you speak. Paste `script.txt` in one go. On first use, check that voice tracking scrolls past tag lines cleanly. Any other prompter app works the same way; if one reads tags aloud or stalls on them, put shot reminders in square brackets instead.

## edit-notes.txt

Three lines, plus any trial marker you are running:

```
HOOK ON SCREEN: <3-5 words, a promise, never the answer>
COVER: <2-4 plain, true words for the thumbnail>
OUTCOME: <what the viewer gets, said plainly>
```

- **HOOK ON SCREEN** shows from the first frame so the video works muted.
- **COVER** is literal and true; the thumbnail is made from a real frame of the video where possible.
- **OUTCOME** is the test of the whole video. Ask "would they want this?" If not, do not make it. Check the hook, the close and the cover against it.
- If you are trialling a variant (say, a different who-am-I tail on a few videos), add a line such as `INTRO: trial` so `platform-data` can compare results later.

Edit notes never go on the prompter.

## Shoot habits (one line each, nothing to plan on the day)

1. **Fluffed a line? Pause a second or two, then restart the whole sentence.** The edit finds every take and cuts cleanly.
2. **Hold a warm look to camera for two or three seconds once,** mouth relaxed and closed. That frame becomes the cover.
3. **Film 30 seconds of B-roll before leaving each spot:** a wide, a detail (hands, tools, a mug, the shop sign; never a customer's document or screen), and a walk in or out of frame.
4. **Second camera or drone running? Let it roll the whole time.** It gives the edit real angle changes and behind-the-scenes material.
5. **After the last line, hold the empty frame for two seconds** (walk out or look away). That is the soft exit.

**Drones:** keep it in sight, clear of people and roads, and follow your local aviation rules (in the UK, the CAA's). Use automated moves for an open, a reveal or a walk, not every beat.
