# Human writing and AI tells

A final-pass checklist for anything the `writing` skill produces: does this read like a person, or like a machine? It sits below the brand profile and `voice-learnings.md`. Where this list conflicts with how the person actually writes, the person wins.

Use it as a spot-check, not a find-and-replace. Swap a word only where the plainer one reads naturally in their voice.

## Why AI copy reads as AI

Three things give it away: predictable word choices, a monotone rhythm, and a handful of tell-tale words and shapes. Fix those and most of the problem goes.

## 1. Vocabulary to swap on sight

| AI tell | Plainer |
|---|---|
| Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally | and, also, what's more, or cut it |
| In conclusion, In summary, Overall | so, the bottom line, or cut it |
| It is important to note / It's worth mentioning | just say the thing |
| Utilise, leverage | use, make the most of |
| Facilitate | help, make easier |
| Delve, dive into | dig into, get into, look at |
| Elucidate | explain |
| Commence, endeavour | start, try |
| Comprehensive, robust, seamless | full, solid, smooth |
| Cutting-edge, game-changer | new, big change, or say what changed |
| Plays a crucial / vital / pivotal role | matters, is key |
| A myriad of | lots of |
| Navigate the complexities of | deal with, get through |
| In today's fast-paced / ever-evolving landscape | delete entirely |

Also on the watch list: tapestry, testament, realm, landscape (figurative), underscore, showcase, foster, intricate, multifaceted, nuanced, garner, boast, elevate, resonate, meticulous, bustling, treasure trove, "aligns with", "when it comes to".

## 2. Shapes and habits

- **Puffery.** "Stands as a testament to", "rich heritage", "leaves a lasting impact", "nestled in the heart of", "vibrant", "must-see". Say the concrete thing instead.
- **Throat-clearing.** "It should be noted that", "no discussion would be complete without". Cut to the point.
- **Tacked-on summaries.** A final paragraph that restates everything, or ends on vague optimism ("the future looks bright"). End on the ask instead.
- **"Not just X, but Y."** The models love it. Use it rarely, only where the person would actually say it.
- **The rule of three.** Endless triplets of adjectives or list items. Use one or two where one or two will do.
- **Every paragraph starting the same way.** Vary openings.
- **Uniform paragraphs and sentences.** All the same length reads as machine-made.
- **Chatbot leakage.** "Certainly!", "Great question", "In this post we'll explore", "Let's dive in", "I hope this helps". Never ships.
- **Vague authority.** "Studies show", "experts say", "many believe" with no source. That's a truth problem as well as a style one: name the source or cut the claim.

## 3. Punctuation and formatting

- **No em dashes.** They're one of the most recognisable tells. Use commas, full stops, brackets or colons.
- Consistent quote marks (don't mix curly and straight).
- Bold sparingly. Bolding key terms mid-sentence reads as generated.
- Emoji only where the audience uses them, and not as bullet points.
- Sentence case for headings unless the brand says otherwise.
- Lists are fine in a caption when the content is a list. Don't turn every thought into bullets.

## 4. Rhythm: vary it hard

AI sentences cluster around 15 to 20 words. People write short ones. Then a longer one that takes its time, winds through a comma or two, and lands somewhere slightly unexpected. After drafting, deliberately split one long sentence and merge two short ones so the rhythm never flatlines.

## 5. Fresher words, still plain

Predictable phrasing reads as AI, so reach for the specific word or the everyday metaphor over the obvious one. But fresh is not florid: "the sun was a searing eye" is purple prose. Aim for the vivid thing a normal person would actually say out loud.

## 6. Leave a little human texture

Flawless, evenly polished prose reads as machine-made. Keep the rhetorical question, the aside in brackets, the contraction, the second thought. A slightly messy sentence that sounds like someone talking beats a perfect one that sounds like no one.

## 7. What not to do

- **Don't route copy through "humaniser" or detector-bypass tools.** They send your content to a third party and can silently change prices, disclaimers and compliance wording. Passing an AI detector is not the goal; sounding like the person is.
- **Don't add em dashes or forced passive constructions** because a guide says they look "human". They don't fit a plain, warm voice.
- **Don't lightly reword an AI draft.** Paraphrasing keeps the machine rhythm underneath. Write from the idea, in the person's voice.
- **Be careful with grammar tools' "rewrite" features.** They smooth a draft back into generic prose. Use them for typos.

## Sources worth re-reading

The Wikipedia essay "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup) is the most thorough public catalogue. It's a publicly editable wiki written for encyclopedia articles, so weigh it rather than absorb it: some of its "signs" (any list, any bold, a friendly tone) are normal and right in marketing copy. Take the lesson, not the literal rule.
