# Interview guide

One batch per turn. Each batch maps to one or two profile sections. Offer the options as a multiple-choice picker where the client supports it, always with an "other / let me type it" choice. Skip anything the user's existing material already answers, and confirm the draft instead.

Keep the tone light. You're a friendly colleague getting to know the business, not a form.

## Batch 1: Identity

- Who appears and posts? (one person / two partners / a team / the brand only, no faces)
- What's the business called, and is there anything else you run that must stay separate from it?
- In one line, what do you do and for whom? (offer to draft it from their site)
- Are you local? If yes, which town or area should content mention?

Follow-up if two people: "Do you each post as yourselves, as well as on a shared brand account?"

## Batch 2: Audience and offer

- Who's your best customer? Describe one real type of person. (Options by business type help: first-time buyers, busy parents, local trades, small business owners, other creators, people in a particular life stage.)
- What are they worried about or searching for when they find you?
- What does an enquiry or sale actually look like? (DM / phone call / booking form / website purchase / walk-in / email)
- What's the main job of social right now? (more enquiries / more sales / bookings / grow an audience / be seen as the expert / recruit)

## Batch 3: Voice

- Paste two or three things you wrote yourself and like: posts, emails, a bio. (This is the most useful thing you can give.)
- Pick up to five that sound like you: warm, plain-speaking, funny, calm, direct, nerdy, cheeky, reassuring, energetic, thoughtful, no-nonsense, other.
- Any words or phrases you'd never use? (jargon, buzzwords, "hustle", "game-changer")
- Spelling: UK English / US English / other.
- Humour: none / light, now and then / a big part of it. Any references that are very you (films, music, sport, hobbies)?

Write traits as "trait, which means..." so they're usable. Example: "Plain-speaking, which means short sentences and no trade jargon unless I explain it."

## Batch 4: Platforms

- Which platforms? (multi-select: Facebook Page, Facebook personal, Instagram, LinkedIn personal, LinkedIn company page, TikTok, YouTube, Google Business Profile, Threads, X, Bluesky, Pinterest, website or blog, email newsletter)
- For each: handle, I or we, and is it posted by hand or through a scheduler?
- Any accounts that must always be posted by hand? (personal Facebook profiles usually are)
- Links: happy to default to "no links, point to the profile" with a short allow-list? (Explain in one line: most platforms now reach fewer people when a post carries a link, so the profile does the converting. The `writing` skill has the detail.)

## Batch 5: Content pillars

Offer a starter set based on the business type, then let them edit. A general small-business set:

- Teach (how-to, myths, mistakes people make): 30%
- Behind the scenes (how you work, what you're building): 20%
- Real stories and results (only true, documented ones): 20%
- Local and community: 15%
- Opinion and reaction (your take on news in your field): 10%
- The offer (what you sell, plainly): 5 to 10%

Ask: which of these fit, what's missing, and roughly what share each should get? Note an example topic per pillar.

## Batch 6: Boundaries

- Anything you never want to be seen talking about? (other businesses, politics, prices, competitors, clients)
- Any claims you never make? (results, guarantees, "best", income, health outcomes)
- Anyone who must never be named without consent? (clients, staff, children, venue owners)
- Anything you always disclose? (your own products, partnerships, freebies)

## Batch 7: Compliance

- Is your business regulated? (yes / no / not sure)
- If yes: which regulator, and who signs off content (you, a compliance team, a network or principal firm)?
- Do you have required disclaimer wording? Paste it exactly. If it varies by type of post, paste each version and say when it's used.
- Do you ever use affiliate or partner links, or get paid or gifted to mention something?
- Where do your reviews and testimonials live (Google, Trustpilot, a spreadsheet of approved quotes)? Can we quote them, and how should people be named?

If they're regulated but have no wording, don't write it. Point them to their regulator's guidance or compliance contact and mark the tier `TODO: confirm with compliance`. The `writing` skill's `references/regulated-industries-example.md` shows what tiered wording looks like for one industry, as a model only.

## Batch 8: CTA and contact

- How do you like to end a post? (a question people want to answer / a plain "message me if you want to talk it through" / an invitation to book / it depends)
- Where does your bio link point? (link hub / website / booking page / shop)
- What contact details can appear in posts? (phone, email, address, website) And on which platforms?

Build a contact block per platform. On platforms that ration or block links, replace link lines with "links on my Page" or "link in bio".

## Batch 9: Visual identity

- Do you have brand colours and fonts? Paste hex codes, or I can pick them up from your website or logo.
- Logo: always / sometimes (where?) / never in content.
- Image style: real photos / illustrated / bold graphic / a mix. Any look you love or hate?

If they have nothing, offer two or three simple palette options to choose from (primary, base, light, accent) with one free headline font and one body font. Record the choice and the reason.

## Batch 10: Likeness

- Do you want to appear in generated images? (yes / no / sometimes)
- If yes: share three to six recent photos, clear face, natural light, at least one in colour. These are only ever used as references.
- What must a likeness get right? (hair colour and length, glasses, beard, build, age range) An honest description beats a flattering one.
- Is anyone else in the photos? Do we have their OK?

## Batch 11: Tools

- Scheduler (Publer, Buffer, Later, Hootsuite, Meta Business Suite, none yet).
- Image model (Gemini image, ChatGPT image, Midjourney, Canva, none).
- Video tools (phone editor, CapCut, Descript, ffmpeg, none).
- Analytics (the platforms' own insights, the scheduler's analytics, Google Search Console, a spreadsheet).

"None yet" is a fine answer. Write it down; the other skills adapt.
