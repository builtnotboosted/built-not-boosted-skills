# Regulated industries: a worked example (UK financial services)

This is an example, not the default. If `## Compliance` in `brand/brand-profile.md` says the business is regulated, its lines win. This file shows how the research standard tightens in one regulated industry so you can see the pattern and apply it to others (healthcare, legal, financial, alcohol, gambling, food claims and so on). Last checked Nov 2026; regulations change, so verify the current rules with the relevant regulator.

## The principle

In the UK, financial promotions (including social posts by regulated firms and advisers) must be **fair, clear and not misleading**, with balanced information about risk, and the firm is responsible even for a throwaway line in a caption. The UK regulator has published specific guidance on financial promotions on social media. Advertising in general must also meet the advertising standards codes.

## What that changes for research

- **Figures need a tier 1 source and a date, every time.** Rates, prices, thresholds and scheme rules move, and quoting a stale one can mislead. Flag every one `VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH` and re-check against the provider's own published terms on the day.
- **Illustrative figures are labelled as illustrative,** in the piece itself, not just in the brief.
- **Hooks keep the tension in a checkable fact, never in a promised outcome.** "This clause in your agreement could cost you thousands" is fine if true. "You'll definitely save", "guaranteed approval" or "everyone should do X now" is the exact pattern regulators flag.
- **Named products and providers:** factual commentary on a provider's real, verified move is fine; never imply a product is the best or only option, or that the business recommends it to everyone.
- **Partisan research:** provider and trade-body research is useful data, but report what was found, not the conclusion the publisher draws, and never present it as an endorsement.
- **Some true figures stay out.** Industry-economics ratios (commissions, loss ratios and the like) can be accurate and still read as a scandal or a verdict when stripped of context. If the source itself does not draw that conclusion, do not let a caption imply it.
- **Provisional is provisional.** Interim reports and consultations are cited as such, with their date.
- **Stay inside permissions.** If the business is not authorised to advise on something, research it only to educate and signpost a referral; never position the person as advising on it.
- **Required disclaimers** live in the brand profile's compliance section. The research brief notes which tier applies so `writing` adds the right one.

## Carrying the pattern to another industry

Ask the same questions: who regulates claims in this field, what claims are banned outright, which figures must be current, what disclaimers are required, and what the business is and is not permitted to do. Write the answers into `## Compliance` via `brand-setup`, and research will follow them.
